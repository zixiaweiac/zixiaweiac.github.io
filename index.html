About

I am a theoretical physicist and junior fellow at the Harvard Society of Fellows.

I work at the intersection of gravity, holography, conformal field theory, and information theory. Previously, I held positions as a research scientist at RIKEN and a research assistant professor at the Yukawa Institute for Theoretical Physics, Kyoto University.

While my primary interest is to understand the fundamental nature of gravity, I draw on a broad range of theoretical tools and apply methods developed through this research also to problems in condensed matter physics, statistical physics, and computation. My current research focuses on three directions: 1. uncovering the nature of gravity in de Sitter spacetime, which provides a useful approximation to our universe; 2. understanding how the presence of multiple measurement devices affects physical systems; 3. exploring the computational power of systems involving gravity. My previous work has explored how the relationship between entanglement and spacetime can shed light on the emergence of geometry, introduced new information-theoretic quantities involving measurement, and used statistical and information-theoretic methods in many-body systems to characterize black hole microstates. I have also applied the holographic principle to study thermalization and critical phenomena in condensed matter systems.
